# Inferior — Lore Reference

> Living lore document. Decisions here inform design and code.
> Where lore solves a gameplay problem, that is noted explicitly —
> lore that does double duty as game mechanics is the best kind.

---

## Guiding principle

All lore in Inferior is **load-bearing**. Nothing is purely decorative. Every worldbuilding decision either solves a gameplay problem, creates a game system, or both. If a lore element doesn't do at least one of those things, it probably doesn't belong here.

## History — the three ages

### First Age: human dominance

Humans were one of the last species to achieve hyperspace capability, but within a few centuries came to dominate and control most of known space. The human empire was not particularly liked — empires seldom are — but it provided a measure of order and, critically, standardisation. The legacy of the First Age is felt everywhere in the Third:

- **SI units** are used across the entire galaxy
- **A version of English** is the de facto lingua franca
- Standardised time, holidays, logistics, and political frameworks trace back to human administration

The empire fell when the **first hyperspace eclipse** cut off all travel and communication. Isolated from the centre, human-controlled worlds could not be held.

### Second Age: chaos and the Postal Service

With hyperspace travel restored but hyperspace *communication* still non-functional, the galaxy fragmented. Only physical transport of goods and messages was possible — a digital letter had to be physically carried. The organisation that came to control this became one of the most powerful entities in the Second Age: the **Intergalactic Postal Service**, which held a monopoly on both hyperspace cargo transport and postal service for several centuries.

The Second Age was characterised by:
- Small unions of worlds, single-world states, and warlords
- Uncontrolled research with little ethical oversight
- Abundant slavery
- Extreme diversity of local law — or absence of it
- Riches and poverty existing in close proximity

Towards the end of the Second Age, **genetic engineering** became a major problem. Superior engineered soldiers were used to conquer planets. Resentment against all genetically modified people — tied to religious and political movements — grew galaxy-wide. The backlash became a serious threat to anyone of non-standard genetics.

The **Bolon** intervened: they evacuated large numbers of modified people to a distant region of the galaxy, providing them with the means to survive and build. There were no habitable planets in this region — they had to construct stations and habitats from scratch.

Then the **second eclipse** hit. The diaspora, now stranded and isolated, developed **short-jump technology** during the eclipse — widely believed to have been assisted by the Bolon. This technology allows travel between stars over days or weeks rather than standard hyperspace speeds, without requiring normal hyperspace access.

### The destruction of Earth

Shortly after hyperspace reopened at the start of the Second Age, **Earth was destroyed** — not entirely, but catastrophically — by the **Thageis**.

During the eclipse the Thageis had rebelled against and overthrown human control on their worlds, killing the humans there. When hyperspace reopened, a faction sent what they called a retaliatory strike: four **Negentropy Bombs** targeted at Earth.

Negentropy Bombs open a portal to a hyperspace plane that extracts energy from normal space, creating a region of negative energy that propagates outward at C. The intent was weather disruption and temperature reduction — a hard blow to the empire's capital, perhaps preparation for an invasion that had not yet been planned.

The new hyperspace technology was not well understood. The bombs detonated between Earth and the Sun rather than at Earth, triggering a **solar eruption** with an avalanche effect orders of magnitude beyond anything anticipated. The result:

- Earth's atmosphere stripped by roughly 50%
- Worldwide floods, storms, fires — a mass extinction event
- 85% of the human population dead
- Enormous loss of species
- The Sun lost several percent of its mass and dimmed noticeably
- **Earth is no longer reachable by standard hyperspace modes** — the damage to the local solar environment disrupted hyperspace access

Earth survived. Humans survived. But the centre of the empire was gone, and the species could not recover its former position.

The **Bolon responded immediately**. Tens of thousands of Bolon vessels surrounded the Thagei homeworld. The planet was invaded. The ruling class, military leadership, and the scientists responsible were executed. A democratic government was imposed. The Thageis have been, in the words of historians, "not a problem" ever since — and have become a broadly constructive participant in galactic relations.

This event established the Bolon's doctrine clearly: they do not prevent. They respond. Swiftly, overwhelmingly, and then they leave.

> *Design note: Earth's inaccessibility by standard hyperspace is a meaningful gameplay detail. Reaching it requires tribal short-jump technology or Bolon assistance — weeks of travel, physically demanding, suitable only for those with strong personal or historical reasons to go.*

### Third Age: the current era

The Third Age begins with the end of the second eclipse and a fundamental shift in how hyperspace is understood. For the first time, research moved from purely empirical ("whatever works") to theoretical. Key developments:

- Better understanding of hyperspace dynamics over time
- Theoretical models capable of explaining the eclipses and reducing the probability of a third
- The **A–Z hyperspace band classification** — systematic cataloguing of all known hyperspace planes
- Understanding of how to program the **Hendar compute layer** (F-band)
- Multiple new modes of hyperspace travel discovered

The consensus among researchers is that a third eclipse is unlikely, though not impossible. Research is ongoing.

Genetically modified humans and hybrid species are now a normal part of the galaxy. "Human" is no longer a pure species. Many genetic variants exist. Being human carries no special status or sympathy — the empire is long gone, and its cultural legacy (English, SI units) is so embedded that it no longer feels particularly human.

## Species & factions

### The Bolon

The oldest known hyperspace-active species, with a recorded history exceeding **one million years**. They originate from a gas giant — a high-pressure atmosphere with no ground surface. Life developed on and around enormous floating **coral reef structures** that drift through the atmosphere. Their biology is adapted to high pressure; they cannot survive in normal atmospheric conditions without protection.

**Physical form and suits:** Bolon spacesuits are a collection of high-pressure spheres joined together, each containing life support, computers, and sensors. They function as small spacecraft in their own right. In space, a Bolon is essentially wearing a ship.

**Bolon vessels** are spherical, matching the form of their suits. During the Thagei response, tens of thousands of them surrounded a planet — a visual that is part of known history and part of why the Bolon are not challenged.

**Doctrine:** The Bolon do not seek control. They observe, and they steer — gently, through ambassadors and influence, away from extremes. They intervene directly only when they deem it necessary, and when they do, the intervention is decisive and final. They then withdraw. They have no interest in occupation or ongoing governance.

**Hyperspace capability:** The Bolon have access to hyperspace bands that no other species has identified or characterised. During both eclipses — events that completely stranded every other civilisation — the Bolon continued to travel and communicate normally. It is widely suspected they also provided or assisted with the technology that ended both eclipses. They have never confirmed this.

**The D3 band:** Signals are occasionally detected on D3 (the hidden D-band sub-band), but no one has been able to decode them. The Bolon connection is speculation — based on the fact that something is clearly transmitting, and no known species uses this band. Various military intelligence agencies are investigating. The Bolon have not commented.

**Status:** Ambassadors present in most major systems. Treated with deference bordering on wariness. Their history is known; their capabilities are not fully understood. Most people would rather not find out what the Bolon consider worth responding to.

### The Thageis

A species with a single homeworld and a colonised moon. Known primarily through their historical role in the destruction of Earth — an act most historians describe as a catastrophic miscalculation rather than a planned genocide. The intent was weather disruption; the result was near-extinction of a species.

Following the Bolon intervention, the Thageis have been under democratic governance ever since and have become constructive participants in galactic relations. The history is well known. Whether and how individual Thageis are regarded today varies significantly by who you ask.

> *Gameplay note: The Thageis are planned as an encounter faction in future development. One homeworld with a colonised moon.*

### The Tribes

The genetically modified and hybrid peoples evacuated by the Bolon at the end of the Second Age. Stranded in a region with no habitable planets, they built a space-bound civilisation from scratch during the second eclipse. Today they are a stable, distinct culture with their own technology, religion, customs, and law.

**Technology:** Short-jump hyperspace drives, developed during the second eclipse. Travel between stars takes days to weeks but does not require standard hyperspace access — giving them an enormous strategic advantage during any eclipse or hyperspace disruption. Their ships are very small and typically unpressurised; crew live in suits.

**Social structure:** Divided between inner tribes (rarely encountered, strict religious and cultural adherence, capital punishment for breaking laws or even customs during trading or first contact — sometimes delivered on the spot) and half-tribals (the primary contact layer with the rest of the galaxy — trading, travelling near-border worlds, and responsible for a portion of criminal activity in border space).

**Military:** Short-jump capability makes tribal ships extremely dangerous despite their small size and relatively weak weapons. The ability to jump short distances in combat — effectively teleporting a ship within a system — creates tactical problems that raw firepower cannot easily solve. Most tribes are peaceful traders. A tribal fleet that is not peaceful is a serious problem.

**Cultural status:** The tribal diaspora, and the circumstances that created it, are part of known history. Attitudes toward tribals vary — from respect and sympathy to suspicion, depending on context and location.

## Hyperspace — structure and classification

### What hyperspace is

Hyperspace is a parallel set of planes that co-exist alongside normal space. It is not a single uniform void — there are many distinct hyperspace 
planes, each with different physical properties. The First and Second Age relationship with hyperspace was entirely empirical: whatever worked, 
worked. The Third Age has introduced the first theoretical frameworks and the systematic A–Z classification.

Hyperspace is not traversable by biological matter in its raw form. All interaction with it is mediated by technology — specifically, by hyperspace crystals.

### The Hendar compute layer (F-band)

All modern computing runs on the F-band — the Hendar layer. Ship computers, station infrastructure, and galactic networks all use this hyperspace plane as their computational substrate, arranged as microservices following the Hendar coding protocol.

Implications not yet fully explored:
- A sufficiently targeted F-band disruption could affect computation across a region
- Systems that deliberately avoid the Hendar layer would be unusual and potentially more resilient to certain attacks
- The Bolon almost certainly have computing infrastructure that does not rely on Hendar

> *Design note: This is a significant lore detail with potential gameplay implications for hacking, EMP-equivalent weapons, and isolated systems. Not a priority for early implementation but worth keeping architecturally in mind.*

### Power bands — multiple competing sources

There are at least four known power generation bands. Ships may use one or more depending on their design and age.

| Band | Name | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| G | X-Power (XP) | Most common. First power band discovered. Name is historical accident. |
| L | Y-Power (YP) | Common. Discovered shortly after X-Power. |
| O | Grey Power | Named for the grey glow of leaks. Less common. |
| U | Lenz Power | Details not yet fully documented. |

Having multiple power source types creates equipment compatibility variation between ships — a ship designed around Y-Power generators cannot simply swap in X-Power components without conversion equipment.

### Hyperspace eclipses

Two eclipses have occurred in recorded galactic history. An eclipse is a galaxy-wide event that cuts off all standard hyperspace functionality — travel, communication, and most technology dependent on hyperspace bands. The cause is not fully understood, but Third Age theoretical models have improved understanding of hyperspace dynamics to the point where a third eclipse is considered unlikely, though not impossible.

During both known eclipses, the Bolon continued to travel and communicate normally, using bands not included in the standard A–Z classification. They are widely suspected to have provided or assisted with the technology that ended both eclipses. They have not confirmed this.

The first eclipse ended the First Age. The second eclipse ended the Second Age. Both were defining catastrophes that reshaped galactic civilisation.

## Hyperspace anomalies

Certain regions of normal space have unusual proximity or permeability to hyperspace planes. These are called **hyperspace anomalies**. They are detectable with specialist scanner equipment but not visible to the naked eye.

Anomalies are significant because they are the origin point of naturally occurring hyperspace crystals: in asteroid fields near strong anomalies, certain mineral formations incorporate hyperspace particles into their atomic structure during formation. This was the first evidence that hyperspace interaction was even possible.

Different anomaly types produce crystals with different properties — the specific hyperspace plane a region is close to determines what gets doped into the crystal lattice. A newly discovered anomaly's crystal properties are unknown until crystals from it have been grown and analysed. **This discovery has genuine economic and scientific value.**

> *Gameplay note: Anomaly detection requires specialist scanner equipment. Crystal farming requires seeding equipment and weeks of in-game time. The loop — survey, seed, return, harvest — creates a career path with no scripting required.*

## Hyperspace crystals

Hyperspace effects are produced by **special crystals excited by laser**. The crystals are themselves hyperspace-doped exotic materials: hyperspace subatomic 
particles have attached to the atomic nuclei of the base material, altering the electron cloud and therefore the material's physical properties.

This creates the "which came first" paradox: producing hyperspace-doped crystals requires hyperspace interaction, but naturally occurring versions exist near 
anomalies. The in-universe answer: anomalies came first. Early researchers found glowing rocks in asteroid fields near anomaly regions, characterised their 
properties, and eventually learned to seed and cultivate them deliberately. The technology bootstrapped itself from natural observation.

Crystals are not interchangeable. Different anomaly sources produce crystals with different affinities. The Graham bands (P, Q, R) are the primary exotic 
particle sources used in crystal and hull element fabrication.

Crystals can be exited by lasers. Hyperspace band and sub band tuning etc can be achieved by tuning the laser parameters:
* Wavelength
* Polarisation
* Incoming angle
* A number of lasers (aka as Vectors), usually one to three, and their relative configuration / angle

This is how the drive and other systems produce their effects.

## The drive

### What it is

Ship engines in Inferior are **not rockets**. No fuel is combusted and ejected through a nozzle. Instead, the drive creates a **standing matter wave** of ionised metal ions. Part of this wave is switched — partially transitioned — through a hyperspace plane (A-band).

This partial switching creates an asymmetry in normal space. That asymmetry produces a thrust vector.

The same mechanism can produce **rotation** via a gyroscopic effect. Ships do not need separate attitude thrusters — all movement, including turning, comes from the drive.

### Fuel and operation

The drive is fed with **metal rods** — reaction mass. The standing wave idles at all times when the engine is on; power is always available. Thrust is controlled by adjusting the wave's **offset**. Increasing offset increases thrust. This means:

- Thrust ramps up — not instant
- Fuel consumption ramps with offset, as matter in the wave becomes degenerate and is exhausted
- The drive can thrust both forward and reverse from the same mechanism

### Degenerate matter exhaust

The drive exhausts **degenerate matter**: normal matter that has had hyperspace subatomic particles attach to its atomic nuclei during its time in the standing wave. This alters the material's properties — it is no longer ordinary matter.

The exhaust exits more like a car exhaust pipe than a rocket plume. Over time:

- Degenerate matter deposits build up on the exhaust — requires periodic cleaning (maintenance loop)
- Under some conditions, **unusual crystals form** in the exhaust deposits — these can be valuable and are a source of rare materials
- The specific crystal type varies with the metal rod material and drive tuning

> *Gameplay note: Exhaust maintenance and crystal harvesting from deposits are recurring loops with predictable cost and occasional unexpected reward.*

### The natural speed limit

The drive aligns itself to the **local gravitational field**. This is the key lore fact that solves several gameplay problems:

1. **Reference frame inheritance:** The ship automatically moves in the reference frame of the nearest large gravitational body — planet, moon, or star. No jarring velocity discontinuity.
2. **Inter-system jumps:** Different star systems move at wildly different absolute velocities through the galaxy. The drive's gravitational alignment means arriving in a new system automatically adopts that system's frame. No physics discontinuity, no arbitrary hand-waving.
3. **Natural speed cap:** In deep space, far from any gravitational body, the gravitational gradient is extremely weak. The drive's alignment becomes unstable and inefficient at high offsets. Efficiency drops sharply — a natural terminal velocity emerges without any governor or hard limit. The player feels resistance, not a wall.

## Alpha red

When **two drives are run together**, they can be configured to produce **alpha red** — a special energy type produced from the A-band interaction between the two drives.

- One engine is configured as `+`, the other as `−`
- Alpha red flows from plus to minus
- It is extremely difficult to contain and nearly impossible to make deviate from the space directly between the two engines
- It tends to leak, creating **mostly harmless red streaks** through air — the visual behaviour of a water hose out of control
- These leaking streaks are a **visible tell of a ship's power state** to anyone nearby

> *Design note: Alpha red is visually striking and has gameplay implications — ship configuration matters, engine placement matters, the leaking streaks are a power state indicator that other players and NPCs can read.*

## Power generation

Multiple hyperspace bands can be used for power generation. The main ship power bus is fed by a generator connected to one or more power bands (G, L, O, or U). These are not interchangeable without conversion equipment, which creates equipment compatibility differences between ships of different origins and eras.

Standard bus power from a generator is distinct from alpha red — different containment requirements, different properties, different uses.

## Shields

Shields draw on the **V-band**, specifically the Alpha and Theta sub-bands. Theta is the superior shield band and is used in higher-quality shield generators. Alpha is more common and cheaper. V-band also supports momentum dampening, which is a related but distinct application.

The J-band (specifically J-b sub-band) can generate **donut vortices** that propagate through space and are effective against shields — an anti-shield weapon type with its own distinct lore basis.

## Exotic materials & hull elements

### How exotic materials are made

Hull elements are produced from **exotic hyperspace-doped materials**. The doping process uses particles sourced from the Graham bands (P, Q, R) — hyperspace particles attaching to atomic nuclei during crystal growth. This produces materials with remarkable properties:

- Extremely strong and lightweight
- Hard to cut or post-process after formation
- Must be produced in **standard sizes** from the outset

Foundries produce elements in standard topological forms — triangles, quads, large quads — because that is what the manufacturing process allows.

### Why ships look the way they do

Ships have simple, geometric designs because **hull elements are produced in standard sizes and are hard to post-process**. This is not an aesthetic choice. Ship designers work within the element grid the same way builders work within brick sizes.

**The low-poly aesthetic is canon.** Players understand intuitively why ships look the way they do without being told.

### Element grades and geography

Not all exotic materials are equal. The specific Graham band anomaly a foundry is near determines what dopant properties are available:

| Grade | Source | Availability |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| Standard | Common anomaly types | Everywhere |
| Military grade | Licensed foundries, specific anomaly types | Restricted, expensive |
| Exotic / rare | Specific known anomaly locations, often contested | Scarce, high value |

### Panel wear and character

Over time, hull elements accumulate wear — micro-impacts, radiation exposure, heat cycling. It is not purely cosmetic: wear tells a story.

- A fresh insurance ship gleams. Veterans find this uncomfortable.
- Mismatched panels of different grades and wear states tell a ship's history at a glance
- Rare cosmetically matched panel sets are something players will seek out
- Losing a ship means losing that patina — a genuine loss beyond credits

## The hyperspace heat sink

Ships do not radiate waste heat into normal space — doing so would produce a large thermal signature detectable by passive sensors. Instead, waste heat is dumped via a **hyperspace heat sink**: a device that transfers thermal energy into a separate hyperspace plane.

Properties:
- Finite capacity — it fills up
- Costs power to operate
- When **saturated**, it dumps heat back into realspace instantly: a massive thermal spike and simultaneous EM burst
- Every passive sensor in range immediately detects the ship

> *Pirate tactic: Force a ship to run silent by maintaining pressure. Wait for the saturated vent.*

## The emergency hyperspace beacon

Every ship carries a **mandatory emergency hyperspace beacon** that:

- Cannot be removed (legal requirement, enforced by ship computer)
- Is single-use, then requires expensive recharge
- Activates automatically on catastrophic hull failure
- Performs one uncontrolled micro-jump to the nearest registered station
- Cannot be triggered voluntarily — requires actual hull failure (safety interlock)

### The tunnel experience

The emergency jump is not the clean hyperspace of normal travel. It is uncontrolled, chaotic, and leaves a mark:

- Geometry that should not exist
- Colours outside the normal spectrum
- Shapes in peripheral vision that vanish when looked at directly
- Brief — roughly 25–30 seconds — then hard white, then a station

The experience costs **sanity**.

> *Design note: The tunnel is the punishment for losing a ship. Brief enough not to push players away, unpleasant enough to be memorable. The grief happens in the tunnel. Arrival is the fresh start.*

## What hyperspace is not

- **Hyperspace is not a transport medium for normal matter.** You cannot ship cargo through hyperspace directly.
- **Teleportation is not available as a general technology.** The emergency beacon is the closest thing, and it is tightly constrained.
- **Hyperspace anomalies are not traversable.** They are regions where hyperspace is close, not holes into it.
- **The Hendar compute layer is not a communication network in the traditional sense.** It is a computational substrate, not a messaging system.
